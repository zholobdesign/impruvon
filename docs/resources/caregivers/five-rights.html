What are the five rights of medication administration?

Right person, right medication, right dose, right route, right time. Check all five, every single pass — and here is what each one actually means when you are standing in front of someone.

  • This article targets the TOFU term 'five rights of medication administration' from the semantic core.

Right person

Confirm who you are giving it to before anything else. A photo in the record, a wristband or a barcode is safer than recognition — especially on a first shift or in a house you are covering.

Right medication

Read the label against the record, not against memory. Look-alike and sound-alike names are the most common source of the wrong drug reaching the right person.

Right dose

Check the number and the unit. Half a tablet and one tablet are different doses; 50 mg and 500 mg are different medications in practice.

Right route

By mouth, topical, injection, inhaled — the route is part of the order. If the person cannot take it the ordered way, that is a call to the nurse, not a decision to make on your own.

Right time

Inside the window the order allows. Early is not safer than late; both are documented, and both matter for medications where timing drives effectiveness.

What happens when the system checks with you

A guided med pass walks these five in order and will not let the record close until each one is confirmed. With MedBox, only the correct drawer opens at the correct time, so the check happens physically as well as on screen.

Common questions

What if I realise afterwards that one of the five was wrong?

Report it immediately, follow your agency's incident procedure and document what happened. A reported error is a fixable error.

Are there more than five rights?

Many agencies teach additional checks — right documentation, right reason, right response. The five are the core; follow your own agency's policy.
